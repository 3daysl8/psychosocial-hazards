# Publication audit arbitration

**Decision date:** 24 October 2026 (Australia/Sydney)  
**Article:** [psychosocial_risk_literature_review.md](psychosocial_risk_literature_review.md)  
**Audits compared:** [gemini-report-audit.md](gemini-report-audit.md) (G) and [argon-report-audit.md](argon-report-audit.md) (A).

**Decision: substantial revision required before publication.** The research supports discussing workplace psychosocial hazards and prevention. It does not validate the proposed calculator, its ranking of hazards, or its numerical reductions and risk bands. Neither audit should be accepted wholesale. Argon's replacement weights are not ready for public use; the Gemini-named audit is more cautious about scoring but also contains errors and overstatements.

This is a source-based editorial and methodological adjudication, not a certification of a clinical instrument or legal compliance. The original article and audits have been preserved. [Proposed publication wording](proposed-publication-corrections.md) and [reference decisions](arbitration-reference-register.md) accompany this report.

## Scope and verification standard

I compared the findings against the local article, queried all 30 DOI-bearing references in its 32-entry bibliography, checked proposed replacement identifiers, inspected accessible research tables and methods, recomputed the arithmetic, and checked current regulator and instrument-owner materials. The first audit's filename describes an audit **of** Gemini research; its header attributes authorship to Claude Code. The filenames are used here without assuming independent provenance or independent verification by the auditors.

The 43 DOI queries and returned metadata are saved in [arbitration_citation_metadata.json](arbitration_citation_metadata.json). Six original DOIs identify unrelated publications; four returned Crossref 404 responses. A registry 404 is a failed identifier check, not proof that an underlying work cannot exist. Metadata verification confirms identity, not a study's numbers, quality or reuse rights. Open-access XML used for table inspection was kept in the system temporary directory, outside the project.

**Accepted** means the stated defect is supported by the source or direct calculation. **Qualified** means the defect is real but the audit's correction or interpretation needs changing. **Rejected** means the proposed correction is unsupported or contradicted. **Unresolved** means the claim must be removed or explicitly withheld pending verification. This exercise is not a new systematic search of all psychosocial-hazard research, and it does not certify every cell of the original evidence table.

## Required publication decisions

| Priority | Decision | What must change |
|---|---|---|
| P0 | Remove false attribution and unsupported permission statements | Delete the invented SWA quotation, rewrite the case-law claim, correct People at Work and PSC permissions, and correct regulator dates/licences. |
| P0 | Withdraw claims of a validated quantitative risk model | Remove the claimed empirical hazard hierarchy, correlation-derived clinical odds ratios, interaction uplift, fixed control reductions and disease-probability interpretations of bands. |
| P1 | Rebuild the evidence table from source-specific records | Keep outcome, exposure contrast, design, adjustment model, effect metric, sample and uncertainty together. Do not transfer statistics between tables or outcome groups. |
| P1 | Replace the bibliography and downstream summaries | Corrections must propagate through the executive summary, hazard narratives, summaries, calculator explanations and any evidence register. |
| P1 | State review limitations honestly | Supply search records if they exist; otherwise describe an evidence-informed narrative review. Remove categorical claims that no research exists. |
| P2 | Standardise presentation | Define OR/RR/HR/correlation, use consistent hazard terminology and remove unsupported quality labels and apparent precision. |

P0 and P1 items are publication blockers. A revised educational article can be published without a numerical calculator; the latter requires a separate development and evaluation process.

### Scoring and evidence synthesis

The following are methodological judgments based on the supplied models and checked studies, not empirically estimated replacement rules.

| ID | Audit cross-reference | Final ruling |
|---|---|---|
| M1 — Correlation conversions | G §4.1/§6.2; A H6/§5.2–5.3 | **Accept exclusion from this calculator, reject hierarchy-based justification.** The conversion equations require assumptions and do not turn continuous, cross-sectional correlations into adjusted prospective clinical ORs. A larger converted incivility value is evidence of non-comparability, not proof that incivility must be less harmful. A's “bridge calibration” supplies neither an estimable model nor validation. |
| M2 — Log scaling | G recommends Option A; A supplies unified weights | **Neither set is validated for site risk.** A log transform is arithmetic, not evidence that unrelated studies' coefficients can be added. ORs, RRs and HRs have different meanings; outcomes, contrasts, populations, adjustment and follow-up differ. Mikkelsen's authors themselves caution against causal inference and direct interpretation of pooled magnitudes. Use its estimates for research discussion or explicitly experimental sensitivity analysis, not certified risk weights. [Mikkelsen discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8159794/). |
| M3 — Universal highest-harm ranking | Article §1/§3.12; A confidence labels | **Remove.** Highest point estimate for one endpoint is not universal hazard severity. Wide CIs, different ascertainment and omitted outcomes prevent the “bullying by far the most damaging across all reviews” claim. Confidence that a hazard matters is distinct from confidence in a numerical weight. |
| M4 — Interaction multiplier | G §4.2; A M7/§5 | **Reject 1.25 and +15%.** Neither is estimated or validated by the sources. G's comparison with 2.98² and 2.98⁴ is illustrative only: a pooled one-exposure category is not the common independent effect of each hazard. Thus it cannot prove submultiplicative interaction either. Increasing exposure count supports attention to co-exposure; establishing interaction requires specific joint contrasts and a defined scale. |
| M5 — Frequency/severity algebra | G §4.3; A M7 | **Reject both formulations as validated.** Adding ordinal ratings is not inherently mathematically forbidden; the problem is their arbitrary scale and untested interpretation. Doubling the minimum score is not, alone, an error if the whole scale is defined consistently. Dividing by two in A's formula does not calibrate it. The proposed benchmark example is also wrong: **1.75 + .55 = 2.30**, not 2.50. |
| M6 — Control reductions | G §4.4; A retains original discounts | **Remove empirical claims and default discounts.** No checked source supports the fixed 10%, 25% or 60% reductions. Confirm implementation and residual exposure through evidence and consultation. Avoid counting a reduction in exposure and then discounting the same benefit again. “Eliminated” and “still present with reduced exposure” require different descriptions. |
| M7 — Band denominator | A M7; G §6.5 | **Accept dilution concern; reject claimed calibration.** Dividing by the maximum across all listed hazards can make a serious isolated hazard look low. A's particular 15–20% estimate depends on weights not fully specified in the article. Its replacement thresholds .60/1.25/2.00 are also unvalidated. Adding and capping scores, as G proposes, does not establish predictive validity. Remove “high probability”, “imminent injury” and compensation predictions. |
| M8 — Severe-event rules | Both audits endorse overrides | **Keep prevention principle; label any software rule as policy.** A serious incident needs action regardless of a total score. Exposure does not establish a diagnosis, and DSM trauma exposure criteria alone do not diagnose PTSD. Do not define severity using a worker's existing diagnosis or treatment as the only trigger for preventive action. |
| M9 — Missing evidence | G §5/§6; A weight floors | **No pooled estimate is not a low-risk finding.** Use “not identified among sources examined” rather than “does not exist”. Remote work, lone work, underload and physical conditions remain assessable hazards. No evidence validates a .18/.20 floor or amplifier-only treatment. |
| M10 — Overlap, double counting and mapping | Additional synthesis finding | **Address before any scoring work.** Reviews can reuse cohorts; a second review is not necessarily independent replication. Job strain overlaps demands/control, ERI is broader than recognition, and bullying/harassment/gendered violence/conflict may describe the same conduct. Document mappings and avoid adding the same exposure through several labels. A's “15-hazard” weight table actually contains **16 rows** because high/low demands are split. |

## Corrections to the audits themselves

The principal arbitration differences are already resolved in the register above. They matter because otherwise a second round of editing would introduce new errors:

- **Argon:** wrong Gerhardt total and replacement authors; unsupported Orwin label; wrong SWA licence; unproved baseline-adjustment counts in two reviews; incorrect Sojo verification; inaccurate People at Work band labels; incomplete Parent-Lamarche authors; unvalidated weights and cut-offs; and the 2.50 benchmark arithmetic error.
- **Gemini-named audit:** Madsen 1.22 exists; Lunen has no “van”; Fox has a plausible real counterpart; Rugulies's least-adjusted estimate is verifiable; comparing exposure-count categories with powers of 2.98 is not an interaction test; and Option A does not establish a validated calculator.
- **Both:** bibliographic correction is incomplete. The original Mikkelsen, Magnusson Hanson and Virtanen author lists need repair; Argon's Duchaine replacement also has incorrect authors. See the reference register and saved metadata.

The Brewin double-slash DOI **resolved to the intended paper** in this check. Prefer the canonical single-slash form, but do not describe that spelling alone as a broken or unrelated DOI. Conversely, correct DOI metadata is not enough to endorse the study's use in the proposed model.

## Instructions for the final draft

1. **Reframe the article.** Present an evidence-informed narrative review of hazards, associations and prevention. Put any experimental scoring proposal in a clearly separate development appendix or remove it from the public version.
2. **Rebuild table rows, not isolated cells.** For every retained estimate record the population, exposure and comparator, outcome definition, follow-up, effect metric, model adjustment, CI, relevant k and N, exact source location, and verification level. Keep review-wide totals distinct from outcome-specific totals. Use “not established” where needed.
3. **Reduce numerical clutter.** Retain a small number of representative, verified estimates with their limitations. Move detailed extraction to a technical appendix. State odds as odds, not percentage increases in probability; correlations are not risk ratios.
4. **Separate three claims:** whether a hazard is recognised, whether a study finds an association, and whether a tool predicts risk accurately. Evidence for the first two does not prove the third. Regulatory action does not depend on establishing a particular pooled effect size.
5. **Preserve practical prevention.** Explain identification, consultation, control implementation and review, using the applicable regulator's guidance. Individual support can accompany work changes. Neither an EAP nor a numerical score demonstrates that a hazard is controlled. [Victorian control guidance](https://www.worksafe.vic.gov.au/compliance-code-psychological-health-part-3-step-3-control-risks).
6. **Recheck the public summaries last.** The current §7 repeats the wrong authors, study totals, causal language and comparative-harm claims. Rewrite from the final corrected extraction, rather than patching names in the old summaries.
7. **State the actual method.** Do not claim searches of Scopus, Cochrane or other databases unless the search records substantiate that work. Report search dates, selection approach and limitations. If AI helped locate material, describe that assistance accurately; do not claim every figure was checked when some were omitted as unverifiable.

## Release criteria

The educational article is ready for editorial release only when P0/P1 defects are corrected, all retained exact numbers have traceable source locations, the bibliography matches the in-text citations, reuse claims reflect the material actually used, and no remaining passage implies validation or legal assurance from the proposed score. Unresolved nonessential statistics can be removed; obtaining every paywalled paper is not a prerequisite to a sound, narrower article.

If a calculator remains an objective, define its intended decision and unit of assessment before choosing a formula. Evaluate it using suitable workplace data, worker/HSR input and relevant expertise. Assess stability under alternative weights, missing responses, overlapping hazards and severe single hazards; evaluate agreement with defensible assessments and, if it claims prediction, calibration and performance on independent data. Until that work exists, no precise replacement weight table is approved by this arbitration.

**Present status:** arbitration and proposed corrections completed; the original article remains unrevised and is **not cleared for publication**.
