# Proposed wording for the public draft

Prepared 24 October 2026. These passages implement the [audit arbitration](final-audit-arbitration.md). They replace the specified claims; they are not a complete rewritten article. Use the [reference register](arbitration-reference-register.md) when rebuilding citations.

## Title and executive summary — replace §1

**Workplace psychosocial hazards: what research can tell us about prevention and assessment**

Psychosocial hazards arise from work and working conditions and can affect psychological and physical health. This review considers research on their associations with mental-health and work-related outcomes, alongside Australian guidance on identifying and controlling hazards. The evidence covers different populations, exposures and outcomes; findings should be interpreted within those differences.

Studies report associations between adverse working conditions and poorer outcomes. Their interpretation depends on the exposure, outcome, study design and adjustment model. Findings for symptoms, diagnosed disorders and sickness absence answer different questions. Observational associations also require caution about causal interpretation. [Rudkjoebing et al.](https://www.sjweh.fi/show_abstract.php?abstract_id=3877&fullText=1).

The reviewed evidence does not establish a validated set of weights or thresholds for a universal workplace risk calculator. Assessment should consider the particular work, workers' exposure, existing controls and their effectiveness. The aim is to identify and address hazardous conditions, including serious individual hazards that could be obscured by an overall score.

## Methods and evidence-table preface — replace unqualified search claims

This is an evidence-informed narrative review. Sources were identified through the available research materials and targeted checks of original publications and official guidance. It is not presented as an exhaustive systematic review. The technical appendix records which findings were checked in full text, which were checked only in abstracts, and which were omitted because adequate verification was unavailable.

Each estimate is reported with its original effect measure and outcome. Odds ratios describe odds, risk ratios describe relative probabilities over a specified period, and hazard ratios compare event rates over time. Correlations describe relationships between measured variables. These measures are not interchangeable. Counts refer to the particular analysis where available; a review's total sample is not necessarily the sample contributing to each estimate.

*Editorial instruction: use this method description only if it accurately describes the work completed. If documented systematic searches exist, report those methods and dates instead. Do not imply that the original AI-generated search narrative establishes that the searches occurred.*

## Selected evidence corrections — replace the affected narrative claims

**Job strain:** Madsen et al.'s unpublished cohort analysis reported HR 1.27 (95% CI 1.04–1.55) in its main model and 1.22 (1.02–1.47) after additional socioeconomic adjustment. Further adjustment for baseline depressive symptoms substantially attenuated the association. The adjustment strategy therefore matters to interpretation. [Madsen et al., Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5471831/).

**Harassment and gendered violence:** Research associates different forms of harmful workplace experiences with poorer well-being. Similar correlations across forms of harassment do not establish equal causal effects for every population or justify assigning identical numerical risk weights. [Sojo et al.](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0361684315599346).

**Traumatic material:** Burnout and secondary traumatic stress are related outcomes. A correlation between them is not an estimate of the increase in risk caused by exposure to traumatic material. Evidence about their association should not be used to calculate an exposure weight. [Cieslak et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23937082/).

**Multiple hazards:** In a Danish prospective study, exposure to more of four adverse psychosocial factors was associated with higher odds of a questionnaire-defined probable depressive disorder. This supports considering concurrent hazards. It does not establish a universal interaction multiplier for a risk calculator. [Lunen et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10567252/).

**Evidence gaps:** A directly applicable pooled estimate was not identified among the sources examined for some broad hazard categories. This is a limitation of this review and the available comparable evidence. It should not be interpreted as evidence that the hazard is harmless or warrants a low assessment priority.

## Assessment and controls — replace §§4 and 6's quantitative endorsements

The evidence in this review does not validate numerical hazard weights, frequency multipliers, control discounts or overall risk bands. The proposed values should therefore not be described as estimates of injury probability or as thresholds for compliance. A recognised survey's benchmarks apply to that survey, scoring method and reference population; they cannot validate a different tool by analogy.

For practical assessment, record the hazard, who is exposed, how exposure occurs, its duration and frequency, the potential for harm, and the controls in place. Consider interacting hazards and check controls against workplace experience and consultation. Record actions, responsibilities and review arrangements. A serious hazard should prompt appropriate action regardless of any aggregate result. [SWA Model Code, assessment and control sections](https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-08/model_code_of_practice_-_managing_psychosocial_hazards_at_work_25082022_0.pdf).

Support services and individual interventions may assist workers, while changes to work address sources of exposure. Their roles are complementary. The presence of counselling, an EAP or training does not by itself demonstrate that a hazard has been controlled. WHO's recommendation for universal organisational interventions is conditional and based on very low-certainty evidence; WHO also conditionally recommends several individual interventions. [WHO recommendations](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK586381/); [Victorian control guidance](https://www.worksafe.vic.gov.au/compliance-code-psychological-health-part-3-step-3-control-risks).

If an experimental tool is retained, place this statement beside its output:

> This tool is an unvalidated aid for organising discussion and follow-up. Its outputs are not probabilities of illness, clinical assessments or findings of legal compliance. Assess and address each identified hazard using relevant workplace information and applicable guidance.

*Editorial instruction: this statement cannot make an otherwise misleading interface acceptable. Remove injury-probability language, automatic empirical-looking discounts and reassuring aggregate labels that conceal serious hazards.*

## Instruments and reuse — replace §5's permission statements

People at Work's survey and online platform were decommissioned on 2 October 2026. Its resources remain available, but the survey is excluded from the platform's general non-commercial copying permission, subject to the specific terms. Availability without a fee did not grant general permission to reproduce survey items in another tool. [Official notice](https://www.peopleatwork.gov.au/); [copyright terms](https://www.peopleatwork.gov.au/assets/pdf/People%20at%20Work%20Copyright%20and%20Disclaimer%20Last%20Updated%2029%20October%202020.pdf).

PSC-12 is a measure of psychosocial safety climate. Its published organisational benchmarks should not be described as diagnostic thresholds for an individual worker. Reproduction of PSC items requires checking the applicable permission: the PSC Global Observatory supplies instructions for applying through APA's permissions process. [Bailey et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25347684/); [permission instructions](https://stresscafe.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Procedure-for-applying-to-APA-Marketplace-regarding-use-of-the-PSC-12-or-PSC-4-Dec-2025.pdf).

For AWB components and other instruments, distinguish discussing findings from reproducing questionnaire items. Identify the relevant instrument, version and permission before reusing protected content. Do not describe public availability or academic publication as a general reuse licence.

## Scope and Australian legal context — replace the personal-factors argument

This guide focuses on conditions of work and does not score a worker's resilience, personality or private life. That is a deliberate scope choice. It does not remove the need to respond appropriately to individual support needs or circumstances.

Australian requirements depend on jurisdiction. Safe Work Australia's model Code requires jurisdictional approval to have legal effect. Victoria has its own OHS framework; its Psychological Health Regulations and accompanying 2025 compliance code took effect on 1 December 2025. Readers should use the requirements and guidance applicable to their workplace. [SWA status guidance](https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/doc/model-code-practice-managing-psychosocial-hazards-work); [Victorian code preface](https://www.worksafe.vic.gov.au/compliance-code-psychological-health-how-use-compliance-code).
