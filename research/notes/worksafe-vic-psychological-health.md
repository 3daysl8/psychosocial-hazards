**Quoted definitions** (WorkSafe tool):

- Psychosocial hazard: "any factor or factors in any of the following: work design; systems of work; management of work; carrying out of the work; personal or work-related interactions, that may arise in the working environment, and cause an employee to experience one or more negative psychological responses that create a risk to their health or safety." (FAQ wording is near-identical.)
- Bullying: "Repeated, unreasonable behaviour directed at a person or group that creates a risk to health and safety."
- Sexual harassment: "A person sexually harasses another person if he or she: makes an unwelcome sexual advance, or an unwelcome request for sexual favours, to the other person, or engages in any other unwelcome conduct of a sexual nature in relation to the other person in circumstances in which a reasonable person, having regard to all the circumstances, would have anticipated that the other person would be offended, humiliated or intimidated."
- Gendered violence: "Any behaviour that is directed at, or affecting, a person because of their sex, sexual orientation, gender, gender identity or because they do not follow socially prescribed gender roles and stereotypes."
- Aggression or violence: "Any incident in which a person is abused, threatened or assaulted in circumstances relating to their work."

Other useful descriptions (summarised): organisational justice has three parts: procedural (decisions made fairly), informational (people given the information they need), interpersonal (treated with dignity and respect). Poor workplace relationships often start as low-level incivility and escalate if unaddressed. Poor environmental conditions include work-provided accommodation.

## Tool structure (useful for Phase 3: objective information)

**Step 1, review internal information.** Sources to check for patterns (who, what, where): sick and unplanned leave; hazard, incident and injury reports; hours worked; meeting and OHS committee minutes; workers compensation claims for psychological injury; complaints and investigation reports (harmful behaviour, ongoing conflict); turnover and exit interviews; EAP usage reports (work-related reasons); industrial relations records such as grievance logs; customer or client feedback (wait times, service levels); productivity records; previous surveys and risk assessments.

**Step 2, observe and consult.** Yes/no questions per hazard, with comments. Any "yes" means a hazard may be present and the risk must be controlled. Must be done in consultation with employees and HSRs.

## Regulations FAQ (summarised)

- **Duties:** employers must provide a working environment that's safe and without risks to health, so far as is reasonably practicable. "Health" in the OHS Act includes psychological health. The Regulations add specific duties to identify hazards, control risks, and review and revise controls.
- **Employees** must take reasonable care for themselves and others, and co-operate with the employer's OHS actions.
- **Victoria's control hierarchy is tailored to psychosocial risk** (Phase 7). Eliminate first if reasonably practicable. Otherwise reduce by altering the management of work, plant, systems of work, work design or workplace environment; or by information, instruction or training; or a combination. **Information, instruction or training can only be used on its own when none of the other controls are reasonably practicable, and must not be the main control in a combination.**
- **Review triggers** (Phase 7), including: new information about a hazard; an employee (or someone for them) reports a psychological injury or a psychosocial hazard; after a notifiable incident involving psychosocial hazards; before changes likely to alter psychosocial risks; when an HSR asks. Full list on WorkSafe site (TO-CHECK the regulation itself).
- **Consultation** (Phase 3): employers must consult employees, so far as is reasonably practicable, on health and safety matters that directly affect them. If employees have an HSR, consultation must involve the HSR. Extends to independent contractors.
- **HSR access to information** (Phase 3 HSR callout): HSRs can access information the employer has about actual or potential hazards and the health and safety of their designated work group. WorkSafe expects this to include information about psychological injuries in the group. No medical information without the person's consent. The Regulations don't change HSR rights.
- **Prevention plans are not mandatory** in Victoria. WorkSafe encourages using its template.
- **Workers compensation is separate:** the Regulations don't change eligibility for workers compensation (WIRC Act 2013) or common law rights. Supports the brief's "processes run side by side" message (Phase 2).
- **Victoria isn't harmonised** with the model WHS laws; wording differs but the overall approach is consistent.
